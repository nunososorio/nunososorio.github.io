How We Work: Think-Pair-Share

Skip the lecture; learn by confronting realistic dilemma scenarios.

1
Think (3 min): Read the case in silence. Write down your individual strategy and note what risks you identify.
2
Pair (4 min): Turn to your benchmate. Compare your answers: Would both of you take the same approach? Where do you disagree?
3
Share (6 min): Plenary discussion with the class and teaching team to formulate the gold-standard scientific approach.
Cycle 1 — 11:55 to 12:10

Case 1: The Longitudinal Data Crunch

The Scenario:

You have just collected raw spectrophotometry and qPCR data from a 6-month longitudinal drug-response assay across 8 cell lines. Your Principal Investigator (PI) suspects these findings are novel and could support an upcoming patent application.

You need to write a Python/R script to normalize the expression levels and generate multi-timepoint curves. Knowing ChatGPT/Claude writes code proficiently, you consider uploading your complete raw Excel spreadsheet with sample IDs, drug concentrations, and replicate readouts directly into the chat prompt to ask: "Analyze this file, compute ANOVA, and give me the script to plot it."

Reflection Prompts

  1. What are the critical risks under University of Minho regulations and international IP laws?
  2. How can you leverage GenAI to write this exact script without exposing confidential bench data?
Faculty Debrief Notes & Key Concepts
  • UMinho Principle of Responsibility & Privacy: Inserting unpublished experimental data or personal details into public GenAI tools is strictly prohibited. Assume any uploaded file trains public models.
  • Solution — Structural Prompting with Dummy Data: Teach students the Role - Task - Goal prompt formula:
    Role: You are an expert bioinformatics programmer in Python (Pandas/Seaborn).
    Task: Write a script to normalize qPCR time-course data across 4 timepoints.
    Context: I am providing 3 rows of synthetic/mock dummy data with the column names.
    Output: Clean Python script with explanatory comments and statistical checks.
  • Use institutional accounts (e.g., Microsoft 365 Copilot with enterprise data protection) when available.
Cycle 2 — 12:10 to 12:25

Case 2: The Phantom Biomarker Citations

The Scenario:

You are preparing the state-of-the-art section of your master's project on a newly identified microRNA biomarker in neuroinflammation. You type into an LLM: "Provide a detailed summary of all recent clinical trials investigating this microRNA, including authors, journal names, and PMIDs."

The AI outputs a convincing, elegantly phrased 3-paragraph summary complete with five formal citations (e.g., "Silva et al., 2024, J. Neurochem, PMID: 38472910"). However, when you search PubMed and DOI databases, two of these papers do not exist, and another has author names completely mismatched to the topic.

Reflection Prompts

  1. Why does a model create "hallucinated" citations that sound completely authentic?
  2. How do model parameters (like temperature) and system architectures (RAG / Web Retrieval) influence this?
Faculty Debrief Notes & Key Concepts
  • Distributional Semantics & Next-Token Prediction: LLMs do not verify factual databases by default; they calculate the statistical probability of words appearing sequentially. A citation "looks" like an author list followed by a year because that pattern is frequent in training data.
  • Model Parameters (Temperature & Top_p):
    • Temperature = 0.0 - 0.2: Low variability, deterministic, best for reproducible tasks and technical parsing.
    • Temperature = 0.8 - 1.0+: High variability, creative, but elevates hallucinations.
  • Mitigation Strategy: Require Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) tools (e.g., PubMed integration, Consensus, Scopus AI) or enforce that the student attaches the verified PDF directly to the prompt.
Cycle 3 — 12:25 to 12:40

Case 3: Scientific Editing vs. Authorship

The Scenario:

You wrote the first draft of your thesis introduction in English. Because English is your second language, you felt the phrasing was awkward. You submitted paragraphs to an LLM with the prompt: "Act as a native English scientific editor. Rewrite this for maximum clarity, conciseness, and high-impact academic tone."

The resulting text is polished and compelling. In doing so, the AI subtly altered one sentence: it changed "Our preliminary in vitro data suggest an association..." to "This study demonstrates a causal mechanism...". You copy-paste the text without noticing this distinction and do not mention AI usage in your declaration statement.

Reflection Prompts

  1. What are the scientific and ethical violations present in this case?
  2. Under Universidade do Minho guidelines, what is the mandatory protocol for declaring GenAI assistance?
Faculty Debrief Notes & Key Concepts
  • UMinho 4 Core Pillars:
    1. Responsibility: The student alone is accountable for false claims (e.g., association vs. causation).
    2. Transparency: AI can never be credited as a co-author. Students must declare the tool, the purpose (e.g., linguistic refinement), and the workflow phase.
    3. Human Supervision: Critical oversight is non-negotiable. Blindly copying text violates academic integrity.
    4. Inclusion & Equity: Legitimate assistance with language parity is recognized, provided it remains transparent and verified.

Universidade do Minho Guidelines (Summary)

European AI Act & UMinho Framework:
  • Confidential Data: It is strictly prohibited to insert sensitive personal data, confidential information, or unpublished research data into public GenAI tools.
  • Authorship: AI tools do not possess legal personality or ethical responsibility; therefore, they cannot be authors of academic articles, works, or dissertations.
  • Modalities of Use:
    • Prohibited: When the activity requires pure, autonomous cognitive performance without technological mediation.
    • Conditioned: Permitted in specific phases of work (e.g., stylistic review, brainstorming) with mandatory critical validation and transparency declarations.
    • Free: Full use integrated into curricular objectives, where AI usage itself is a competence being developed.
